What worked

  • Carousels beat video. 10 carousels averaged 61.2 likes and 12.9 comments vs 41.2 likes and 4.0 comments for 29 videos. Carousels drew 3x the comments.
  • Press and milestone posts are the top category. Just 3 posts (Eyewitness News feature, Top 25 ranking, 2012 news report) averaged 91.3 likes and 21.7 comments, far above every other category.
  • Personal story is the reliable engine. 19 personal posts averaged 51.2 likes and 6.6 comments. The top 5 by engagement are all personal or press, not listings.
  • October was her strongest month. 18 posts in October (5 per week for four straight weeks) vs 11 in August and 8 in September. Cadence doubled and held.

What didn't

  • Listing posts underperform. 11 listing and sold posts averaged only 35.5 likes and 3.5 comments, the weakest category except tips.
  • Buyer tip reels get likes but no conversation. 6 tip and market posts averaged 28.0 likes and just 2.3 comments.
  • Conversation is concentrated. Only 4 posts drew 10+ comments, while 11 posts drew 0–1 comments. Most of the audience watches without replying.
  • Lowest post: the October 22 development follow-up reel, 11 likes and 2 comments, a story follow-up with no standalone hook.

Suggested next experiments

  1. Turn every listing into a carousel with a personal frame. Listing posts are the second-weakest category, but carousels are the strongest format. Pair the next listing with a personal slide (why this home, what the seller's story taught her) and measure likes and comments against the 35.5 / 3.5 listing baseline.
  2. End tip reels with a question. Tip reels earn likes but almost no comments. Adding one direct question in the caption or final frame tests whether conversation lifts from the 2.3-comment baseline. Recheck after 4 tip reels.
