Dirt to Dream:
579 Shore Drive

Raw land to move-in, month by month.

1
Months
1 to 4

Design and Permits

What happenedThe lot is cleared on paper before it's cleared in person. Architectural plans, engineering, septic design, and permit applications. This phase feels slow and it's the most important one. Every hour spent here saves ten in the field.

Homeowner mindset: Excitement mixed with impatience. This is when having a builder who communicates matters most.

2
Months
5 to 6

Site Work and Foundation

What happenedClearing, driveway, well, septic, foundation pour. The lot transforms fast. For the first time, you can stand where your kitchen will be.

Homeowner mindset: It gets real. The money starts moving and the abstract becomes concrete.

3
Months
7 to 8

Framing

What happenedWalls go up, roof goes on. The house takes shape in weeks. This is the most visually dramatic phase and the most fun to visit.

Homeowner mindset: Pure excitement. Bring friends. Take photos. This is the phase everyone remembers.

4
Months
9 to 10

Rough-Ins

579 Shore Drive photo: Phase 4
Photo being added from the build archive

What happenedPlumbing, electric, HVAC, insulation. Invisible work that determines how the house lives. Not photogenic, absolutely critical.

Homeowner mindset: “Is anything happening?” Yes. The most important things happening are the ones you can't see.

5
Months
11 to 14

Finishes

What happenedDrywall, flooring, millwork, tile, counters, fixtures, paint. The house becomes a home. Selections you made months ago arrive and get installed. This is where finish level shows.

Homeowner mindset: Decision fatigue is real. A builder with a clear selection process carries you through this.

6
Months
15 to 16

Final Stretch

What happenedPunch list, final inspections, certificate of occupancy, deep clean, walkthrough. The last 5 percent takes real focus.

Homeowner mindset: So close. A good builder doesn't coast here.

Sixteen months from first sketch to keys. No drama, no disappeared contractors, no mystery invoices. That's what a real process looks like.
